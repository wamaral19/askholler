Problem
As an ecommerce brand, the data we get about our customers and their decision making process is poor. It’s been (mostly) garbage in, which has led to a bunch of decisions that are “data backed”, but not actually correct.

This leads to inefficient ad spend, suboptimal brand positioning, and poor attribution for our marketing team. Our product team deals with similar problems - they don’t get great feedback which makes most of our merchandising decisions intuition based with a lot of guesswork.

Solution
Holler’s team of humans talks to your customers at specific points in their journey. For example, immediately after checkout is completed on your ecom store a customer will get a text asking if they’re open to a quick call for feedback and chat with a researcher.

Only certain customers are targeted based on their characteristics. These segments combined with discovery questions based on hypothesis are “Angles”. For example, one angle might be “repeat brand purchasers who are purchasing within a new product category for the first time”, or “customers attributed to Google”.

Using Shopify webhooks, a customer is dropped into the research queue immediately post purchase to be contacted and interviewed. The interview is recorded, and researchers are prompted to ask questions based on the Angle. For example, if the angle is focused on attribution the researcher will ask the customer if they remember where the customer originally heard of the brand, if they looked at competitive products at all, and where they went to buy the product. Given they’re human to human conversations, if the customer goes off on a tangent or wants to talk about a key driver that wouldn’t be captured by survey this information will be captured and shared with brands.

Holler delivers these insights to brands in Angle Reports. If a brand is looking for more general research rather than proving or disproving a specific hypothesis they’ll receive research on a monthly basis.

Section 2: Product & Deliverables

What are Research Angles?

Research Angles are specific questions or hypotheses that a company wants to investigate with a defined segment of customers. Examples of Research Angles could be…
New customers purchasing tops
Repeat customers purchasing a new category
Customers returning an item and subsequently making another purchase
Customers with an increase in AOV and increase in items from purchase one to purchase two
Customers attributed to a specific advertising channel

Each Research Angle has a set of qualification rules that determine which customers should receive calls. The Research Angle also determines which questions researchers should prioritize during the conversation.

Why do we prioritize breadth over depth in customer interviews?

We believe two things matter: sample size matters, and the best in depth conversations come randomly within a larger sample rather than asking for interviewees to dedicate time.

We believe that the majority of the value of a customer conversation comes during the first three to five minutes. For many of the questions marketers want answered, including brand resonance, attribution and competitive positioning, we don't believe we need 15 to 20 minutes of conversation.

We expect the median conversation to last approximately five to seven minutes.

There will be variability.

For a sample of 100 conversations, we might see:
10 conversations lasting approximately 20 minutes
80 conversations lasting approximately five minutes
10 conversations lasting approximately two minutes

The longer conversations might uncover particularly interesting customer anecdotes or provide additional depth around a specific topic.

We want our researchers to pursue those conversations when they're valuable, but we aren't designing the product around generating long interviews.

Our default approach is to maximize the number of customer conversations while maintaining a high standard of research quality.

10. Why do we use human researchers instead of AI interviewers?

Our number one priority is that the customer must walk away with a more positive impression of the brand relative to what they had prior to the conversation. We don’t believe that AI will accomplish this.

At some point there might be a tradeoff between the cost of a human and the cost of compute to make it more cost effective to conduct the interview with AI and deliver something to the customer of value. We don’t think we’re at that point yet, and it’d get into payment for interview territory which we believe creates bias in the sample.

AI is a tool for our human researchers to be more efficient and help them in their conversation.

11. Do we help companies implement the recommendations from our research?

Not today. If it becomes a clear request of our customers in the future we might. We want to be focused on delivering user insights and allowing brands to make choices.

Why is Holler valuable on a recurring basis rather than as a one time research project?

CMOs and marketing teams will always have hypotheses about their customers, and we’re a way to validate or disprove those hypotheses. The hypotheses will evolve over time as they learn more and gather new data and insights, but they’ll still have questions.

Working with Holler on a recurring basis generates coverage - missing out on an insight is costly.

Why would a customer continue paying during months where the insights are less valuable?

Companies don't know which research engagements will generate the most valuable insights before conducting the research.

For example, a company might begin researching a specific customer segment with the expectation that they'll uncover something relatively minor, only to discover a major opportunity. The opposite can happen as well. We believe that the opportunity cost of not conducting customer research is significant enough that companies will want to continue investigating their hypotheses on a recurring basis.

Section 3: Pricing & Packaging

12. How does pricing work?

Internally, we’re thinking there’s a fixed cost to get started, a fixed cost for the delivery of a report, and variable costs per interview.

If we assume the average interview is 7 minutes with 3 minutes of latency between interviews (calls that aren’t answered, manual work that we’re yet to automate, etc), a researcher can complete 6 interviews per hour. We’d estimate they can complete 5 hours of interviews per day without quality slipping, meaning we expect to get 30 interviews per researcher per day. Assuming a researcher makes $60k annually, we’re roughly getting to a variable cost per interview of $8 per interview.

We’ll assume that the upfront setup takes ~2 hours (including a 30 minute intake call, 90 minutes of data analysis) and producing the report takes ~4 hours. Assuming those resources cost $120k per year, at approximately 2,000 working hours annually, we’re looking at an hourly cost of $60. That translates to $120 for upfront setup and $240 for report production, or $360 in fixed labor costs per research engagement.

Combining these costs, our estimated total cost per engagement is $360 + $8 per completed interview. At 30 interviews, that’s $600 total ($20 per interview); at 50 interviews, $760 ($15.20 per interview); and at 100 interviews, $1,160 ($11.60 per interview). We expect these costs to come down as our process gets tighter and more uniform.

Section 4: Sales & Onboarding

17. What is the sales process?

We plan to initially offer a free research engagement lasting approximately three to seven days.

The objective is to demonstrate the quality of our research before asking a company to commit to a paid engagement.

The process will work as follows.

Day 1
Company installs the Holler Shopify app.
Holler receives access to the relevant Shopify data.
Holler conducts an initial analysis of customer behavior, segments and available attribution information.

Day 2
Onboarding call with the CMO or relevant marketing leader.
We review our initial analysis and discuss the hypotheses that the company wants to investigate.
We propose Research Angles that we believe could generate useful insights.
We begin conducting customer interviews.

Day 3
Deliver initial customer conversations, transcripts and tags.
Conduct a short check-in to determine whether the information is useful.

Day 7
Deliver the initial Research Angles report.
Discuss the insights generated and the opportunity to continue working together.
Customer decides whether to move forward with a paid subscription or individual engagement.

Day 30
First full Disco for customers that continue.
Review the report within two to three days of delivery.

We expect to offer this free research engagement during the early stages of Holler.

As we develop customer references and a stronger reputation, we hope to eliminate the need for free trials.

18. Why offer a free trial rather than asking customers to pay up front?

Right now, we don’t have any customers to rely on as a reference point or anchor point for the value we’ll provide. Without that anchor, it’s tough for us to establish pricing and to land sales. As we gain traction, case studies, and proof we’ll peel back trials.

For now, we’re hoping that we can demonstrate enough value in seven days to help customers make a decision about continuing. If we learn that we’re not that valuable over 7 days we might extend the period.

19. Who is the primary buyer?

We expect the primary buyer to be the CMO or another senior marketing leader.

These are the people responsible for making decisions about customer acquisition, marketing spend, positioning and growth.

We also expect product teams to find the research valuable, particularly when investigating product feedback and repeat purchase behavior.

Our initial sales process will focus on marketing leaders.

Section 5: Research Operations

20. What are we guaranteeing customers?

We guarantee completed interviews and a digestible report.

21. What happens when a Research Angle doesn't have enough volume?

We'll evaluate the feasibility of each Research Angle before beginning the engagement. Given order history, we can forecast roughly how many orders of that type they’ll receive on a daily basis. This forecast will enable us to set up realistic expectations about timelines. We’ll build in a 25% buffer on timelines by default.

23. What happens when customers don't answer?

We'll need to make additional outreach attempts to reach the promised number of completed interviews.

The economics of the model will depend on response rates, the time required to conduct successful interviews and the amount of additional operational work required.

We'll need to understand these variables through our early engagements.

24. What makes a conversation count as completed?

A conversation is complete when at least one of the questions for the Angle is answered. Angle questions can be anything from “how did you hear about the brand” or “which competitive products did you consider, if any”. We have to have at least one question answered, it can’t be a basic demographic question.

Each Angle will have questions that can then have answers segments and tagged, which will help in data visualization delivery.
